02. Ingredient Literature

2.1 Soft-Shelled Turtle Peptides

Research Focus: Exercise Endurance, Energy Metabolism, and Oxidative Stress Markers

A study published in Foods in 2022 investigated the relationship between soft-shelled turtle peptide supplementation, exercise endurance, energy metabolism, and oxidative stress markers in mice.

Research Findings
  • The supplemented groups demonstrated an approximately 35.4%–57.1% increase in swimming time to exhaustion.
  • Changes were observed in selected serum biochemical markers associated with exercise-related fatigue.
  • The researchers reported changes in indicators related to energy metabolism.
  • Increased antioxidant enzyme activity and reduced levels of selected oxidation-related markers were also observed.

These findings provide experimental information on the relationship between a specific soft-shelled turtle peptide preparation and exercise-related physiological responses in an animal model.

RESEARCH TYPE: PRECLINICAL ANIMAL STUDY

Reference: [1]

2.2 Maca (Lepidium meyenii)

Research Focus: Human-Reported Outcomes Under Different Supplementation Conditions

Maca is a plant native to the Peruvian Andes, where its root has traditionally been consumed as food.

Several human intervention studies have examined different maca preparations in selected participant populations.

Gonzales et al. (2002)

This investigation involved healthy adult men receiving 1.5–3 g of maca daily over a 12-week study period.

Research Findings
  • Statistically significant changes in a specific male-reported assessment measure were observed at weeks 8 and 12.
  • Approximately 40.0% and 42.2% of participants demonstrated an increase in the assessed subjective measure at the respective time points.
  • The observations were not clearly associated with changes in circulating testosterone concentrations.

Zenico et al. (2009)

This randomized, double-blind study examined a defined clinical population receiving 2.4 g of maca extract daily for 12 weeks.

Research Findings
  • Changes were observed in selected clinical questionnaire scores.
  • Differences were also reported in subjective well-being assessments.

Stone et al. (2009)

This study involved male athletes receiving 2,000 mg of maca daily for 14 days.

Research Findings
  • Changes were reported in selected subjective assessment measures.
  • Physical performance was also evaluated.

Together, these studies contribute to the scientific literature concerning maca preparations under specific human research conditions.

RESEARCH TYPE: HUMAN INTERVENTION STUDIES
Study Limitations The studies differed in participant characteristics, sample sizes, preparation types, intake amounts, supplementation periods, and assessment methods.

References: [2–4]

2.3 L-Arginine

Research Focus: Amino Acid Metabolism and Selected Blood-Flow-Related Measurements

L-arginine is an amino acid involved in several normal physiological processes.

It participates in protein metabolism and serves as a precursor within the nitric oxide synthesis pathway.

In 2022, Menafra et al. published a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical study in the Journal of Endocrinological Investigation.

The study examined a high-dose L-arginine supplementation protocol in a defined clinical population.

Research Findings
  • The intervention involved 6 g of L-arginine daily for 3 months.
  • Statistically significant changes were reported in selected clinical assessment scores among certain participant subgroups.
  • Changes in selected hemodynamic measurements were also observed in some subgroups.
  • Results differed across participant subgroups, and some blood-flow-related measurements did not demonstrate statistically significant changes.

The study provides clinical research data concerning L-arginine supplementation under defined research conditions.

RESEARCH TYPE: RANDOMIZED, DOUBLE-BLIND, PLACEBO-CONTROLLED HUMAN CLINICAL TRIAL
Study Limitations The participants belonged to a specific clinical population, and the study employed a high-dose intervention of 6 g per day.

Reference: [5]

2.4 Zinc

Research Focus: Essential Mineral Nutrition and Associations with Biological Markers

Zinc is an essential trace mineral involved in numerous normal biological processes, including enzyme activity, protein synthesis, and cellular function.

In 2009, Colagar et al. published an observational human study in Nutrition Research, comparing zinc concentrations in biological samples from different male participant groups.

The study examined associations between zinc concentrations and selected biological measurements.

Research Findings
  • Seminal plasma zinc concentrations were significantly higher in the fertile participant group than in the infertile comparison group (p < 0.001).
  • Positive statistical correlations were reported between seminal plasma zinc concentrations and selected sperm-related laboratory measurements, including sperm count and normal morphology.
  • Associations were also observed between zinc concentrations and selected other mineral measurements.
  • Differences in zinc concentrations were additionally examined in relation to smoking status.

The findings describe statistical relationships between zinc concentrations and selected biological indicators within the studied population.

RESEARCH TYPE: HUMAN OBSERVATIONAL STUDY

Reference: [6]
